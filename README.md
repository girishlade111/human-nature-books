## Book Guide

### 1. Thinking, Fast and Slow — Daniel Kahneman
Nobel laureate's landmark on judgment and decision-making.
**Core ideas:** System 1 (fast, intuitive) vs System 2 (slow, logical), cognitive biases, heuristics, loss aversion, framing, overconfidence.
**Best for:** Behavioral economics, decision science, critical thinking.

### 2. Man's Search for Meaning — Viktor E. Frankl
Psychiatrist and Holocaust survivor's memoir + introduction to logotherapy.
**Core ideas:** Meaning as the primary human drive, suffering and resilience, freedom of attitude, purpose in adversity.
**Best for:** Existential psychology, resilience, philosophy of life.

### 3. Behave — Robert M. Sapolsky
The biology behind every human behavior — from one second before an action to millennia of evolution.
**Core ideas:** Neuroscience, hormones, genes, culture, adolescence, war and peace, free will, morality.
**Best for:** Neuroscience, biology of behavior, nature vs nurture debate.

### 4. Games People Play — Eric Berne
Founder of Transactional Analysis on the hidden social games we all play.
**Core ideas:** Parent-Adult-Child ego states, life scripts, social transactions, unconscious relational patterns.
**Best for:** Social psychology, relationships, communication patterns.

### 5. On Human Nature — E. O. Wilson
Pulitzer-winning classic on the evolutionary roots of human behavior.
**Core ideas:** Sociobiology, kin selection, altruism, aggression, religion and ethics as evolved traits.
**Best for:** Evolutionary psychology, anthropology, ethics.

### 6. Pre-Suasion — Robert Cialdini
Predecessor/companion to *Influence* — what you do *before* you persuade matters most.
**Core ideas:** Attention and association, privileged moments, framing, trust, timing of persuasion.
**Best for:** Persuasion, marketing, negotiation, communication.

### 7. Predictably Irrational — Dan Ariely
Why we systematically make irrational economic and everyday decisions.
**Core ideas:** Relativity of pricing, zero cost effect, ownership bias, expectations, dishonesty, procrastination.
**Best for:** Behavioral economics, consumer psychology, everyday decision-making.

### 8. Sapiens — Yuval Noah Harari
A brief history of *Homo sapiens* — from insignificant apes to rulers of the world.
**Core ideas:** Cognitive Revolution, myths and shared stories, money/empires/religion, Agricultural and Scientific Revolutions.
**Best for:** Big history, anthropology, culture and civilization.

### 9. The Blank Slate — Steven Pinker
The case against the idea that the mind is a blank slate.
**Core ideas:** Human nature and genetics, denial of innate traits, violence, gender, politics, violence and progress.
**Best for:** Cognitive science, nature/nurture, philosophy of mind.

### 10. The Laws of Human Nature — Robert Greene
18 laws decoding human motives, biases, and irrationality.
**Core ideas:** Self-awareness, empathy, purpose, conformity, aggression, envy, grandiosity, change.
**Best for:** Self-mastery, leadership, reading people, practical psychology.

### 11. The Lucifer Effect — Philip Zimbardo
From the Stanford Prison Experiment — how good people turn evil.
**Core ideas:** Situational power, systems and authority, deindividuation, conformity, heroism as resistance.
**Best for:** Social psychology, ethics, authority, crowd behavior.

### 12. The Selfish Gene — Richard Dawkins
Gene-centered view of evolution that reshaped modern biology.
**Core ideas:** Replicators and vehicles, kin selection, altruism, ESS, memes as cultural replicators.
**Best for:** Evolutionary biology, genetics, origins of cooperation.

### 13. The 48 Laws of Power — Robert Greene
Classic (and controversial) treatise on power dynamics through history.
**Core ideas:** Strategy, status, manipulation and defense against it, historical maxims, court politics applied to modern life.
**Best for:** Strategy, history, organizational dynamics, power literacy.

2. Thinking, Fast and Slow
3. Predictably Irrational
4. Man's Search for Meaning

**Path B — Deep Human Nature / Science:**
1. The Selfish Gene

3. The Blank Slate
4. Behave
5. Sapiens

**Path C — People, Persuasion & Power:**
1. Games People Play
2. Pre-Suasion
3. The Laws of Human Nature
4. The 48 Laws of Power
5. The Lucifer Effect

**Single-book quick win:** short on time? Read *Man's Search for Meaning* (short, profound) or *Predictably Irrational* (fun, practical).
